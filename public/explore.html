Where could AI help in my everyday work?

Start with one task you repeat: drafting a routine reply, organizing notes, or preparing a checklist. Write down the steps and decide which parts require your judgment.

Try this: Give an AI tool a made-up example and ask it to draft a checklist. Compare its answer with how you actually work. Correct anything missing before using it.

Keep customer details and confidential information out of your example. Keep critically thinking for yourself when using AI.

Why do people visit my website and leave?

A visitor needs to understand who you help, what problem you address, and what to do next. A clear page gives them a useful answer before asking for a commitment.

Try this: Read your opening screen on your phone. Can you name the audience, the service, and the next step without scrolling? Ask someone unfamiliar with your business to do the same.

This exercise can reveal confusing wording; it does not prove why every visitor leaves. Use actual visit and inquiry data when available.

How can I follow up without pressuring people?

Begin with what the person asked for. Give one helpful answer and one clear next step. Respect a request to stop and avoid sending repeated pitches.

Try this: “You mentioned [specific need]. Here is [relevant resource]. If it would help, you can [one next step].” Use this only when it reflects a real conversation.

Automation can organize reminders and drafts. A person should review sensitive replies and any commitment involving price or scope.
