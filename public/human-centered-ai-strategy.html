A practical approach

Start with the work before choosing the technology.

1. Clarify the outcome

Define what needs to improve: time, response speed, consistency, access to information, appointments, or service quality.

2. Map the workflow

Identify repeated steps, handoffs, delays, and decisions that are creating friction.

3. Set human boundaries

Decide which judgments, approvals, sensitive conversations, and commitments stay with people.

4. Select the smallest useful tools

Use existing systems where possible before adding subscriptions or building unnecessary complexity.

5. Test in real work

Measure whether the system is actually saving time, improving communication, and helping people do better work.

6. Improve responsibly

Keep ownership clear, review outcomes, and adjust the system as the organization learns.

Where this applies

Strategy, automation, AI agents, and Digital Twins.

The same principle applies whether you are improving a CRM follow-up process, connecting Google Workspace, building an AI assistant, creating a Digital Twin, or training a team.

Good AI implementation should make it easier to:

  • Find and understand information
  • Follow up consistently
  • Reduce repetitive administrative work
  • Prepare people for better conversations
  • Escalate sensitive or high-impact decisions to a human
